## Bachelor of Education Policies

### Bachelor of Education: Academic Load Policy

The Bachelor of Education is an accelerated 16-month after-degree program in which students will move through the program as a cohort. Students in the BEd after-degree program who complete the required courses as scheduled are considered full-time.

### Bachelor of Education: Academic Standing Policy

#### Purpose and Scope

The Academic standing policy is to ensure the quality of the program and educational outcomes of the curriculum are met and one of the ways CMU will ensure graduates are qualified and capable teachers and educators.

#### Definitions

CMU: Canadian Mennonite University

BEd: Bachelor of Education

#### Guidelines

To remain in good academic standing in each semester of the education program, students achieve a minimum grade of C+ in all education courses (EDUC prefix).

Students who fail to achieve a minimum grade of C+ will normally be permitted to repeat a given course only once and may not repeat more than 6 credit hours of education courses in the entire program. If this occurs, a student will be dismissed from the program. Failure to achieve a minimum grade of C+ may also delay starting a practicum placement until the next practicum block and delay graduation. If students have need to retake more than 6 credit hours of education courses and they have compelling reasons for why this is the case, they are encouraged to speak to their advisor.

If a student does not meet the above academic grade requirements for continuance in the education program, the student will be dismissed from the program. The Chair of Education will confirm the dismissal in writing (via email) and copy the Registrar on the correspondence.

#### Reinstatement After Dismissal

Reinstatement into the education program is not guaranteed and a student must apply for reinstatement. A student may encounter two different dismissal scenarios:

· A student has been dismissed from the education program and may continue studying at CMU in another program.

· A student has been dismissed from the education program and has been suspended from the university.

##### Reinstatement after Dismissal from Education

In cases where a student has been dismissed from the education program and may continue studying at CMU, the reinstatement process is as follows:

1. The student may apply to the Chair of Education in writing for reinstatement after a period of one year from the point of dismissal. The appeal must be made a minimum of one month in advance of the intended start date. In the written application, the student should indicate that they understand the reasons for their dismissal and articulate why they are now prepared to carry out their studies in good academic standing. The Chair of Education will consult with the Registrar regarding overall academic performance. The Chair of Education will consult as appropriate but the decision to reinstate the student into the Education program remains the prerogative of the Chair.

2. If reinstatement is granted, the student will return with the standing of Conditional Continuance. During the period the student has this standing, the student’s instructors will monitor the student’s performance. If the student completes this period and achieves satisfactory grades, the student’s status will change to Satisfactory Standing.

##### Reinstatement after Dismissal from Education and CMU

In cases where a student has been dismissed from the education program and suspended from the university, the student must first be reinstated to CMU prior to appealing for reinstatement to the education program. See CMU’s Academic Standing Policy regarding process for reinstatement to CMU. If the student is reinstated to CMU, the student will then follow the process identified above to seek reinstatement to the education program.

#### Appeals

If a student believes they have been inappropriately dismissed or denied reinstatement without just cause, that student has the right to appeal to the Academic Student Issues Committee within fifteen days after receiving notification. The appeal will be directed to the Registrar. The student may appeal on the following grounds:

· Where process as outlined in this policy was not followed, to the detriment of the student.

· Where consequences were disproportionate, or otherwise out of line with policy and past practice.

· Conversely, the argument that minimum academic requirements have been attained will not be considered for grounds of appeal.

Academic Student Issues Committee will consider particulars the appeal and evidence provided by the student as well as information from the Education department. The decision of ASIC is not subject to further appeal. The chair of ASIC will inform both the student and Chair of Education the appeal decision and the rationale.

### Bachelor of Education: Admissions Requirements and Procedures Policy

#### Purpose and Scope

The Bachelor of Education at Canadian Mennonite University (CMU) is an after-degree program. The following will clarify the admissions requirements and what is expected of applicants as they apply for admission from the point of submitting said application. Further, it will detail the process of determining who will be offered a letter of acceptance.

#### Definitions

CMU: Canadian Mennonite University

BEd: Bachelor of Education

AGPA: Adjusted Grade Point Average

EDI: Equity Diversity and Inclusion

** **

#### Principles

Admission of applicants to the Bachelor of Education program is guided by the admission standards of CMU and the education requirements set out by the Teacher Certification Board. The number of applicants admitted each year depends on the number of qualified applicants applying and the available resources within the education program. An initial screening for admission is based on Adjusted Grade Point Average (AGPA). Applicants who meet the minimum AGPA will be screened based on their personal statement, relevant work/ volunteer experience, 2 letters of reference (one work/volunteer and one academic) and, if applicable, an indication of equity. Successful applicants at this point will be required to participate in an interview as part of the admission process.

#### Guidelines

Key to the admission process is the determination of successful applicants by the following:

1. Submission of completed education application before closing date.

2. AGPA of 2.5 based on the most recent 60 credit hours of university-level study

3. Evidence of suitability for the education profession.

#### Admission Requirements

Applicants must meet all Canadian Mennonite University and program admission requirements.

· A recognized Bachelor’s degree conferred prior to beginning the program

· A minimum adjusted grade point average of 2.5 (C+) based on the most recent 60 credit hours of university-level study

#### Teachable Requirements

The admission requirements outlined by the Teacher Certification Board do not limit admission to specific bachelor’s programs or specify teachable requirements. CMU follows the guidelines for admissibility as set out by the Teacher Certification Board. However, we strongly advise students considering teaching in high school to take courses in their teaching area(s) of interest. Students considering teaching in early or middle years should consider courses in a variety of areas including mathematics, the sciences, English, history, and courses in their teaching areas of interest.

#### Equity in Admissions to Education

CMU aims to promote equitable access to and strives for greater representation of under-represented groups in Canada. Applicants to the education program are eligible for Educational Equity under CMU’s Equity Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) commitment (see cmu.ca/research-office/public-accountability). At the admissions stage, equity consideration will be given to applicants who identify as an EDI priority group or as a former child in care. Membership in one or more of the following equity groups will result in an adjustment according to EDI principles.

· **Canadian Indigenous People**: This includes Canadian First Nations, Inuit, Métis peoples.

· **Black and People of Colour**: This includes people who belong to communities that have historically experienced systemic racism. This category includes non-Canadian Indigenous peoples.

· **Persons with Disabilities:** This includes people who have a diagnosed physical, mental, psychological, sensory, or learning disability.

· **2SLGBTQIA+ Persons**

· **Economic Marginalization:** This includes people who have been excluded from participating fully in the economic life of their communities, leading to significant disparities in wealth and opportunity.

· **Former Child in Care:** This includes people who were in the care, custody, or guardianship of a child welfare organization.

#### Admission Procedures

Once the applications go live and until the deadline for submission, the Admissions department will track and ensure applicants are supported in completing their individual applications for consideration. The total pool of applications is initially reviewed by Admissions staff and completed application files are referred to the Education Admissions Review Committee.

**A student who meets the minimum admission requirements is not automatically assured of an offer of admission**. A set number of students will be admitted to the Bachelor of Education program and this number may vary from year to year.

##### Application Review

The application packages will be ranked in order of the review scores using rubrics and a select number of applicants will be invited to interview for a spot in the program based on the following criteria:

· AGPA: Score out of 12.5

· References: Score out of 12.5

· Work & Volunteer: Score out of 12.5

· Personal Statement: Score out of 12.5

The application packages will be ranked in order of the review scores.

##### Interview

A select number of applicants will be invited to interview for a spot in the program. The interview with the Education Admissions Review Committee will be 20 minutes in duration. The committee will independently score each applicant, engage in discussion about each interview, and arrive at consensus as to the final scores.

##### Admission Decision

Final admission to the BEd program will be weighted accordingly:

· Application package score (AGPA, Academic References, Work & Volunteer, Personal Statement): 50%

· Interview score: 50%

· Applicants who identify as one or more of the EDI categories outlined above will have their score adjusted according to EDI principles.

The final scores will be communicated back to the Admissions team who will prepare letters of acceptance along with copies of the Education Professional Readiness and Suitability Policy and the Academic Standing Policy which will constitute both acceptance and understanding and receipt of the attached policies via signature. These will be sent out to the top selected applicants. In the event there is room for additional seats in the program (either due to attrition, rejection of acceptance or concerns at the interview stage), the next highest scored applications will be invited for additional interviews and assessment.

#### Admittance

· The Education department will not defer any admissions to the program. If a student cannot begin studies in the term for which they are admitted, the student will need to reapply in a future year.

· A student must accept the offer for admission to secure a place in the program.

· All students who have accepted the offer for admission are required to submit a Child Abuse Registry and Criminal Record Check to the Education department prior to the start of the program.

### Bachelor of Education: Degree Completion Policy

#### Guidelines

The maximum time for completion of their Bachelor of Education is four years from date of the start of courses within the Bachelor of Education program. Any approved leaves of absence are counted within the four years.

#### Procedures

While students are encouraged to review their student enrolment status, the Registrar at CMU will review each new semester and notify students within 2 weeks of their being at risk of not meeting the requirements for program continuance. Students are encouraged to seek out their academic advisor to discuss how to complete their program of study within the four-year period.

If students do not meet the Requirements for Continuance in the education program via having reached the four-year limit, termination is confirmed by the Chair of Education in writing, a copy of the correspondence is sent to the student as well as to the Registrar. Refer to the *Bachelor of Education Academic Standing Policy* for further information.

Students dismissed from the education program for failing to achieve a grade of C+ in more than two education courses will not be readmitted to the program. Students who are dismissed for other reasons may reapply to the program after twelve months. Refer to the *Bachelor of Education Academic Standing Policy* for further information.

The BEd is a 16-month intensive program. Failure to achieve a grade of C+ in any one education course may delay the student’s ability to enroll in the next practicum course or continue in the program for that given year. A delay in course continuance for this reason will be included in the four-year time limit.

### Bachelor of Education: Professional Readiness and Suitability Policy

#### Purpose and Scope

All Bachelor of Education teacher candidates are expected to demonstrate attitudes, values, and conduct that create safe spaces for peers and students. At CMU and in the school setting, teacher candidates are expected to maintain professional behaviour consistent with The Manitoba Teacher’s Society (MTS) Code of Professional Practice during classroom participation, in written assignments, presentations, and K-12 school contexts.

Teacher candidates are expected to be independently familiar and current with all policies and procedures including, but not limited to: The MTS Code of Professional Practice; and relevant University and Practicum policies, and school policies, regulations, and procedures.

All teacher candidates at CMU are required to comply with course regulations, the policy on harassment and discrimination, academic appeal and dismissal procedures, and other relevant policies. Teacher candidates enrolled in the CMU education program have additional responsibilities regarding personal and professional suitability through compliance with specific education policies included in the CMU Academic Calendar and in the BEd Student handbook.

#### Definitions

CMU: Canadian Mennonite University

BEd: Bachelor of Education

EDSC: Education Suitability Committee

MTS: Manitoba Teacher’s Society

#### Guidelines

A professional readiness and suitability review is to be applied when a concern is raised that a teacher candidate is failing to maintain suitability for professional education practice. This includes but is not limited to evidence of a violation of the MTS Code of Professional Practice, or of either the Federal and/or Manitoba Human Rights Act. A review process is conducted if incidents are persistent (i.e., despite documented feedback from faculty/teacher mentor) and/or in the case of serious unethical behaviour (i.e. violation of the MTS Code of Professional Practice). The following is a non-exhaustive list of circumstances that may raise concerns about professional suitability (whether or not they have caused harm) leading to initiation of a review process:

· Attempts to gain advantage or deceive, through falsification, forgery, or misuse of any record or document, or through false statements or representations including but not limited to academic integrity. Any academic misconduct instances in the university classroom setting will fall within the purview of CMU’s Academic Standing Policy.

· Imposing stereotypes on a student, peer, teach-mentor, and or member of the community, including behaviour, values, or roles related to race, ethnicity, religion, marital status, gender, sexual orientation, age, socio-economic status, income source or amount, political affiliation, disability or diagnosis, or national origin, that would interfere with the provision of education to the student or create an unsafe classroom.

· Persistent and/or serious inability to form a professional, teaching relationship based on an empathetic understanding of another person’s experience.

· Harassment, or any threat, intimidation, or attempt to harm another person or student.

· Engage in behaviours that are detrimental to the physical, emotional and mental well-being of others including peers, instructors, teachers, and students.

· Engaging in behaviours that are a conflict of interest or exploiting a relationship with a student for material, ideological, or other gain.

· Substance use that interferes with a teacher candidate’s ability to function within a professional context.

· Serious and/or persistent conduct that is in direct violation of any CMU policy that exists to protect the safety and/or well-being of all persons involved in the body politic of CMU, including staff, students and faculty.

· A conviction due to a violation of the Criminal Code of Canada, which demonstrates lack of judgment and integrity, including, but not limited to, physical assault, sexual assault, human or drug trafficking.

· Concealment or distortion of the truth on the Application for Admission to the CMU Education program. This is considered academic misconduct under CMU’s Academic Integrity policy.

· Persistent and or/serious conduct that contravenes the policies of the practicum setting (applies to teacher candidates in practicum).

A professional readiness and suitability review may also be applied if there is a physical or mental condition which impairs essential performance-including academic standards needed to achieve appropriate teacher education, recognizing that reasonable accommodation for the special needs of individuals is required by the Manitoba Human Rights Act.

#### Limits of Confidentiality

CMU Education program must balance the need for confidentiality against its duty to protect present and future students or persons who might otherwise be placed in jeopardy by a teacher candidate who is acting in an unprofessional or unethical manner. Information disclosed during meetings with instructors, program or practicum coordinators and teacher mentors, or the chair will not be kept confidential if the information raises concerns about the teacher candidate’s capability of assuming the professional responsibilities of the education profession. Instructors and/or program coordinators and/or the chair will share pertinent information with each other in a respectful and professional manner, for the purpose of identifying teacher candidate issues and enhancing problem solving about the concerns. CMU Education reserves the right to share information with the University or third parties as required by law and University regulations.

#### Basic Components of Professional Suitability Cases

Every case involves a decision about whether a teacher candidate’s actions have not demonstrated professional readiness/suitability, and, if so, what are the appropriate consequences.

1. **Assessment**: Information is gathered

2. **Decision**: This decision is to be made by adjudicating the evidence gathered and arguments made by the instructor or teacher mentor, and the teacher candidate, according to a balance of probabilities

3. **Consequences**: Appropriate consequences depend on context and follow certain patterns. Further guidance is given below, under the heading “Consequences.”

#### Procedure I: Assessments

There are three stages of review in CMU’s Education program regarding behaviour that is not consistent with the recognized ethics and standards of the profession of education. The stage of review is dependent upon the seriousness of the allegation of the breach of this policy. The overall commitment of the Education department is to resolve any concern related to professional conduct of a teacher candidate, at Stage One or Stage Two through a mediation approach with the teacher candidate. Records of any incidents that are subject to the review process will be placed in the teacher candidate’s file by the Chair of Education.

When a concern about a teacher candidate’s professional behaviour first arises, it is preferable for the instructor to make first contact with the teacher candidate about the issue. Thus, all professional readiness and suitability procedures begin with the instructor/teacher mentor **gathering evidence**, **consulting with the Chair of Education**, and **meeting with the teacher candidate**, though the aims of that meeting differ depending on the context.

1. **Gather Evidence**: when there is reason to suspect a concern about a teacher candidate’s professional behaviour, the instructor/teacher mentor, begins to gather evidence. If there is enough evidence, the instructor/teacher mentor proceeds to a consultation with the Chair of Education.

2. **Chair of Education**: The instructor/teacher mentor consults with the Chair of Education to review the situation, confirm that there is reason to suspect a professional behavioural concern, and receive guidance about how the case might be best approached. The case is then diverted to stage one, two, or three.

##### Stage One: Instructor/Teacher Mentor Assessment and Meeting with the Teacher Candidate

1. **Meeting with Teacher Candidate**: After gathering sufficient evidence, and consulting with the Chair of Education, the instructor/teacher mentor meets with the teacher candidate to discuss concerns regarding the behaviour. In some cases, an instructor may request the support of the Chair of Education in meeting with the teacher candidate. If the instructor is unable to meet with the teacher candidate, the instructor must provide sufficient information to the Chair of Education to warrant a meeting. The meeting should take place as soon as reasonably possible, to reduce the likelihood of continued behaviour. The instructor will make all reasonable effort to contact the teacher candidate. If the teacher candidate does not respond or refuses to engage, a decision will be made in the teacher candidate’s absence. If the problem arises in practicum, the practicum instructor will discuss the concerns directly with the teacher candidate and Faculty advisor and will follow the procedures outlined in the Field Education Manual for "**Teacher Candidates Experiencing Difficulty in the Practicum Classroom".** This corresponds with Stages one and two of the three stages of review for Professional Suitability.

1.1. **Meeting Goals**

1.1.1. **To gather evidence and better understand the situation.** The purpose of the meeting is to gather and share information about the behaviour. The instructor will note their concerns, share what evidence they have, and ask the teacher candidate for an account of the matter. The teacher candidate will have an opportunity to share any evidence or material they feel is relevant. If the teacher candidate has additional evidence, they must submit it within 24 hours of the meeting. The instructor will document the dates and content of all meetings with the teacher candidate.

1.1.2. **To decide whether unprofessional behaviour has occurred**. The instructor will determine whether unprofessional behaviour has occurred based on information gathered and according to a balance of probabilities. The instructor may communicate the decision to the teacher candidate in the meeting. In cases where it is not completely clear that unprofessional behaviour has occurred, the instructor may postpone a decision to allow for additional consideration or further consultation with the Chair of Education.

1.1.3. **To determine next steps**.

1.1.3.1. If the concerns are resolved at stage one, no further action is taken.

1.1.3.2. If the concerns are not resolved at stage one, the individuals will proceed to stage two.

##### Stage 2: Mediation

If the concern expressed by an instructor cannot be resolved at Stage One, a Stage Two review involves mediation.

2. **Mediation Meeting**

2.1. **Meeting Participants**

2.1.1. The instructor (or representative). If the instructor is unable to attend, they should submit in writing (e.g., by email) a statement of their concerns.

2.1.2. The teacher candidate’s faculty advisor. In situations where the instructor and faculty advisor are the same, the Chair will appoint an alternate faculty member.

2.1.3. The teacher candidate. The teacher candidate may invite a support person to the meeting, whom the teacher candidate may consult, and who may ask questions of clarification, but will otherwise not normally speak on behalf of the teacher candidate. All reasonable effort will be made to contact the teacher candidate for the meeting. If the teacher candidate does not respond or refuses to engage, a decision will be made in the teacher candidate’s absence.

2.2. **Meeting Preparation**

2.2.1. A statement of the behavioural concern and all relevant files for discussion (if applicable) will be circulated to all participants of the meeting one business day in advance. This will include the nature of the concerning behaviour accompanied by all evidence, with time for all to review. If the teacher candidate has additional evidence, they must submit it to the faculty advisor two business days in advance of the meeting.

2.3. **Meeting Goals**

2.3.1. **Determine the nature and specifics of the concern.** All participants will have the opportunity to present and respond to evidence and ask questions.

2.3.2. **To decide whether concerning behaviour has occurred and develop a plan to address the concern.** If the concerning behaviour is substantiated, the instructor will work with the teacher candidate and advisor to develop a written plan (including timelines and specific actions) to address the concern. A written copy of the plan will be emailed to all parties and each person must respond via email agreeing to the plan of action.

2.3.3. **To determine next steps**.

There will be a follow up meeting with all parties to determine if the concern has been resolved.

2.3.3.1. If the concern has been resolved, no further action is required.

2.3.3.2. If the plan of action has not been followed or if concerning behaviour persists, the individuals will proceed to stage three within two weeks of the follow up meeting.

##### Stage Three: Major Concerns

A Stage Three review is conducted when a teacher candidate is being considered for dismissal from the program or if a recommendation for suspension from the University is being considered. A stage three review be conducted in one of two instances:

· Behavioural concerns have not been resolved in stage one or two of the concerns process.

· Behavioural concerns are considered by the Education program to be of such a serious nature that formal intervention is required immediately.

Within two weeks of being advised, the Chair will convene an in-camera meeting of the Education Suitability Committee (EDSC). During the review process, a teacher candidate may be expected to abstain from attending class, practicum, or other school activities until this has process has been completed. Any decision is based on a majority vote of the EDSC at Stage Three.

3. **Education Suitability Committee (EDSC) Meeting**

**3.1. EDSC Members**

3.1.1. The Chair of Education or designated representative.

3.1.2. Two designated CMU Education Faculty members of alternates if necessary. A member of the committee that has had prior experience or contact with the teacher candidate shall not be disqualified due to this as a sole rationale

3.1.3. A representative from Student life or Registrar’s Office, normally the Coordinator of Academic or Student Advising.

3.1.4. A member of MTS external to CMU may be invited to attend depending on the nature and severity of the concerns.

3.2. **Meeting Participants**

3.2.1. The EDSC members as outlined above.

3.2.2. The teacher candidate. The teacher candidate may invite a support person to the meeting, whom the teacher candidate may consult, and who may ask questions of clarification, but will otherwise not normally speak on behalf of the teacher candidate. All reasonable effort will be made to contact the teacher candidate for the meeting. If the teacher candidate does not respond or refuses to engage, a decision will be made in the teacher candidate’s absence.

3.3. **Meeting Preparation**

3.3.1. A statement of the behavioural concern and all relevant files for discussion (if applicable) will be circulated to all participants of the meeting one business day in advance. This will include the nature of the concerning behaviour accompanied by all evidence, with time for all to review. A copy of the Professional Readiness and Suitability Policy will also be provided. If the teacher candidate has additional evidence, they must submit it to the Chair of Education two business days in advance of the meeting.

3.3.2. It is the obligation and responsibility of the Chair of the EDSC to communicate to the teacher candidate the possible consequences of the review process, including the potential for sanction, and to notify the teacher candidate of the membership of the EDSC. To this end, the teacher candidate should know that they are able to raise concerns or objections to any member related to any bias or conflict of interest. They should know that the committee member will have an opportunity to respond to this concern and is either expected to recuse themselves based on their conversation with the chair or they may remain if this concern has been deemed not be significant or warranted.

3.3.3. The chair must communicate to the teacher candidate regarding their right to a support person, the process and rights related to an appeal as well as their rights to know the information that is being presented against them.

3.4. **Meeting Goals**

3.4.1. **Determine the nature and specifics of the concern.** All participants will have the opportunity to present and respond to evidence and ask questions.

3.4.2. **To decide whether concerning behaviour has occurred and determine consequences.** If the concerning behaviour is not substantiated, the teacher candidate will continue in the program. If the concerning behaviour has been substantiated, the EDSC will determine consequences as outlined below. Any decision is based on a majority vote of the Committee at Stage Three. A written copy of the decision will be emailed to the teacher candidate.

#### Consequences

When discussing the range of possible consequences, the instructor, Chair of Education, and the EDSC will consider the following factors in Determining Consequences:

a. The severity and the extent of the behaviour.

b. Whether the teacher candidate has displayed similar behaviour in the past.

c. Whether there is evidence of honest misunderstanding of expectations, or of an intent to deceive or do harm.

d. Whether the teacher candidate indicates a willingness to admit errors and learn.

e. The year in which the teacher candidate is enrolled and level of studies.

f. The experience a teacher candidate has with Education expectations.

g. The pedagogical needs of the teacher candidate.

h. Past precedent.

**Consequences in Cases assessed in Stage One and Two**

The hope in Instructor meetings is that a case reaches a resolution in which the teacher candidate and the instructor (as well as any other relevant parties) mutually and voluntarily agree upon a path toward repairing any harms and achieving the relevant pedagogical goals. Occasionally such resolutions are not forthcoming, and instructors will have to assess consequences unilaterally. Typical consequences assessed by the instructor may include:

a. A warning.

b. Setting goals, actions, timelines.

c. Monitoring.

d. Mentoring and Support.

**Consequences in Cases assessed in Stage Three**

The hope in the EDSC meetings is still that the case resolves with the teacher candidate better oriented toward achieving the relevant pedagogical goals. Typical consequences in stage three cases may include:

a. Any or all of the consequences outlined in stage one and two.

b. Placing the teacher candidate on conditional continuance in the program and monitoring behaviour within the program.

c. Referring the teacher candidate to counselling.

d. Requiring the teacher candidate to temporarily withdraw from the program.

e. Full dismissal of the teacher candidate from the Education program.

f. EDSC cannot suspend a teacher candidate from the university but can make a recommendation to the Registrar and Dean of Student Life.

#### Dismissal from the Education Program

When a teacher candidate displays multiple instances of concerning behaviour and seems unwilling or unable to learn from their mistakes, or have displayed serious unethical conduct, they are not suitable for the Education program. EDSC may decide that such teacher candidates should be dismissed from the program.

1. **Considerations for Dismissal**

1.1. The level and number of behavioural concerns and the likelihood of committing future inappropriate behaviours.

1.2. Violation of the MTS Code of Professional Practice.

2. **Report**

2.1. Where a dismissal has been assessed, the Chair of Education notify the teacher candidate in writing, including a brief account of the reasons for dismissal. A copy of the letter will be sent to the Registrar and placed in the teacher candidate’s file.

3. **Dismissal**

3.1. If the dismissal occurs prior to the voluntarily withdrawal date, the teacher candidate will be allowed to voluntarily withdraw from their courses.

3.2. The teacher candidate’s academic transcript will indicate behavioural misconduct as the basis for dismissal from the program.

4. **Reinstatement After Dismissal**

4.1. Reinstatement into the Education program is not guaranteed.

4.2. The teacher candidate may apply to the Chair of Education in writing for reinstatement after a period of one year. The appeal must be made a minimum of one month in advance of the intended start date. In the written application, the teacher candidate should indicate that they understand the reasons for their dismissal and articulate why they are now prepared to carry out their studies in good standing and are unlikely to commit further unethical behaviour. The Chair of Education will then interview the teacher candidate in a meeting that includes another Education Faculty member for consultative purposes. The Chair of Education will consult as appropriate but the decision to reinstate the teacher candidate remains the prerogative of the Chair.

4.3. If reinstatement is granted, the teacher candidate will return with the standing of Conditional Continuance. During the period the teacher candidate has this standing, the teacher candidate’s instructors will monitor the teacher candidate’s behaviour. If the teacher candidate completes this period and achieves Satisfactory Standing, that teacher candidate may appeal for the removal of the notation regarding dismissal from their transcript.

#### Appeals

If a teacher candidate believes they have been inappropriately sanctioned for ethical and behavioural conduct, that teacher candidate has the right to appeal within fifteen days after receiving notice of the sanction.

1. Appeal of decisions at Stage One and Two will be directed to the Chair of Education. The chair will review the evidence in consultation with the concerned parties: teacher candidate, instructor, teacher mentor, others as appropriate. The Chair of Education will make a decision on whether the consequences are appropriate.

2. Appeal of a decisions made by the EDSC at stage three will be directed to the Vice-President Academic.

**Appeals to the VPA**

If a teacher candidate believes they have been inappropriately sanctioned for ethical and behavioural conduct by the EDSC, that teacher candidate has the right to appeal within fifteen days after receiving notice of the sanction. The appeal will be directed to the Vice President Academic.

1. **Grounds for Appeal**

1.1. Where process as outlined in this policy was not followed, to the detriment of the teacher candidate.

1.2. Where consequences were disproportionate, or otherwise out of line with policy and past practice.

1.3. Conversely, the argument that unethical or inappropriate behaviour has not occurred will not be considered grounds for appeal.

2. **Appeals Process**

2.1. **Intent to Appeal**. Teacher candidates will notify the Chair of Education of their intent to appeal within 15 days of EDSC's decision report.

2.2. **Statement of Appeal**. Within a reasonable timeframe, the teacher candidate will follow the notice of intent to appeal with a written *Statement of Appeal* addressed to the VPA, that includes:

2.2.1. A request for the VPA to hear the teacher candidate’s appeal.

2.2.2. A summary of the EDSC's decision, and any other relevant history and context.

2.2.3. A rationale for the appeal.

2.3. **Statement Review**. The VPA will determine, on the basis of the *Statement of Appeal*, whether or not to hold an appeal hearing. The VPA will hold an appeal hearing if it seems on the face of it that there are grounds for appeal.

2.3.1. If the VPA determines that there do not appear to be grounds for appeal, the VPA proceeds to step **VPA Report**, and notifies the teacher candidate and Chair of Education.

2.3.2. If the VPA determines that there appear to be grounds for appeal, the VPA proceeds to step 2.4. **Appeal Meeting.**

2.4. **Appeal Meeting**

2.4.1. **Meeting Personnel**

2.4.1.1. The VPA.

2.4.1.2. The Chair of Education, as a representative of the existing decision and consequences.

2.4.1.3. The teacher candidate. The teacher candidate may invite a support person to the meeting, whom the teacher candidate may consult, and who may ask questions of clarification, but will otherwise not normally speak on behalf of the teacher candidate.

2.4.1.4. A member of MTS external to CMU.

2.4.2. **Meeting Preparation**

2.4.2.1. The VPA may request from Chair of Education any files relevant to the case, including both materials pertinent to the case at hand, as well as any other files related to past teacher candidate conduct pertaining to suitability in the program.

2.4.2.2. All members of the meeting should have copies of all relevant files for discussion circulated at least 24 hours in advance (including both teacher candidate conduct files and the teacher candidate’s *Statement of Appeal*).

2.4.3. **Meeting Goals**

2.4.3.1. To determine whether there are good grounds to appeal that should result in an overturning of EDSC’s decision or a modification of consequences.

2.5. **VPA Report**

2.5.1. The decision of the VPA is not subject to further appeal. Regardless of the outcome, the VPA will notify the teacher candidate and the Chair of Education in writing (via email), including the following information:

2.5.1.1. The appeal decision.

2.5.1.2. The rationale for the decision.
